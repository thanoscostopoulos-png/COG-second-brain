The DJ set becomes a test case. All those affirmations on Jan 31 are attempts to hold a different narrative while walking into a validation-threatening situation. And it seems to have worked, at least partially. On Feb 1, you don't obsess over whether people liked it. You focus on what you gained and who showed up for you.

**What I notice**: The validation hunger seems most intense with people whose approval feels uncertain (Jovana) and less present with people who consistently show up (Bo, Mike, Dimos). This connects to the pattern you discover on Feb 3.

## Pattern: Relationship Investment Asymmetry

This is the new insight of the week, articulated on Feb 3. You describe an exercise from *The Connection Playbook* where you identified someone you trust (Ilias) and someone you trust less (Jovana), then noticed how you treat each:

"I realised how sometimes I could be complacent with Ilias, whereas with Jovana I could spend a lot more energy trying to make sure the relationship won't break."

You speculate: "People I trust are considered 'sealed cases' in a way: i know they will be there and I don't need to fight for it, prove myself worthy of them being there, hence I am more laid back and often put less energy in the relationship. On the other hand, with people I trust less, I feel then need to compensate and present myself in such a way where I can win them, so I can trust them...?"

You're cautious about generalizing, noting that Ilias lives in Athens and you've known him over a decade, so there are contextual factors. But you write: "might be a good idea to keep looking for this pattern. In fact i want to keep looking for this pattern." Then, playfully: "(I wonder if the ai agent will catch this in a later exercise)."

The week provides evidence for this pattern. On Feb 1, you write about your friends showing up to the DJ set with genuine emotional force: "fucking legends, I am moved by all of them." But these are presumably people you already trust. The emotional reaction suggests you might not have been expecting it—or perhaps you'd taken their support for granted until it was demonstrated so tangibly.

Meanwhile, Jovana appears multiple times across the week (Jan 28, Jan 29, Jan 30, Feb 3) as a source of anxiety about validation. On Jan 29, you mention telling her about your app idea and "she gave me a very lukewarm response." You process this at length, wondering if she doesn't believe in you, or can't express support, or doesn't understand the importance of supporting someone's creative will.

**What I notice**: The people who showed up for you (Bo, Mike, Dimos) get gratitude. The person whose response was lukewarm (Jovana) gets analysis, rumination, and continued mental energy. The asymmetry is playing out in real time across these pages.
